# Strong Fire rebuild — handoff, 4 August 2026

Assume you have none of the context from the session that produced this.

## Where it stands

Live on strongfire.ca, **not indexed** (deliberately — see below). 508 pages, up from
~50. Ten commits on `rebuild-desktop-publication`, pushed, deployed to production.

The site was a phone-only PWA locked to a 512px column, with a separate Python-rendered
news section dumped into `public/` that nothing linked to. It is now one site with a
desktop shell, the brief rendered through its own layouts, and several surfaces that
did not exist.

## What was built

- **Desktop shell.** Masthead above 768px, phone app unchanged below. CSS switches; no JS.
- **Figure registry.** 21 rates/amounts in `src/data/figures/2026.json`, each with source
  and review date. The build fails on stale ones. Fixed live errors: CCB two years stale,
  GST credit replaced by CGEB and still showing $533, GIS $18 low.
- **Nations section.** `/nations` (today), `/archive`, `/ledger` (42 records, filterable),
  `/open` (166 open questions), `/bc` (all 201 BC First Nations), `/who`.
- **Search** across all 508 pages, built from rendered HTML, precached for offline.
- **Glossary** split from one page into 89.
- **Reach.** Sitemap, robots, canonical, Open Graph, share card. Every page has its own
  description.
- **`/what-applies`** — seven money situations, what decides them, what is settled, what
  is not, and which kind of professional to ask.
- **Landing page** — news down one side, directory down the other.

## Things that were wrong and are now fixed

Worth knowing because they show the failure modes:

1. The Indigenous Leadership Circle was described on the live site as an RBC employee
   network. It is independent. Same error was in personal memory.
2. Two of three "CRITICAL" items in the monthly audit were false positives. Applying them
   would have put real errors into a live tax tool.
3. A first pass attributed ledger records to 198 of 201 BC Nations — normalising "Nation"
   away left empty-string keys that match everything.
4. 33 pages shared one meta description, including the ones people search for.
5. Search ranked glossary stubs above the full guides.

Four of those five were found by **looking at the live site in a browser**, not by
reading code. That is the lesson.
